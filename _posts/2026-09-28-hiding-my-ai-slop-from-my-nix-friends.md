I am [using AI a lot]({% post_url 2026-05-31-ai-is-a-boon-for-the-anal-retentive %}). I am public about my use of it and when required make the necessary disclosures: "Assited-By" trailers or having to say "I used an AI to help write this software" _on every_ [Discourse](https://discourse.nixos.org/) post.[^discourse]

[^discourse]: Seems like whether or not you use AI, I feel more compelled just to disclose I did to avoid the "you didn't disclose it" argument. Such is life.

<!--more-->

For those of you in the "Anti-AI" crowd, it might surprise you to learn that those of us who enjoy using it also don't want to read "slop". I also don't want anything I create to _read_ like I used AI a lot. Commit messages, READMEs, code comments. I'm reading it also!


Being a Nix person, my instructions to the agent are of course declarative. My `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` (and Codex's `AGENTS.md`) is generated by home-manager from [agent-settings.nix](https://github.com/fzakaria/nix-home/blob/master/users/fmzakari/agent-settings.nix). It has a whole section titled _"Writing style: no LLM tells"_ that I stitched together from Wikipedia's [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) and Simon Willison's [LLM cliche highlighter](https://tools.simonwillison.net/llm-cliche-highlighter).

```nix
writingStyle = ''
  ## Writing style: no LLM tells

  ### Banned vocabulary

  Do not use these words. They are statistically the loudest signal of
  machine-written text: delve, tapestry, meticulous, pivotal, intricate,
  ...
'';
```

The code-style tries to ban many _AI-isms_ as they emerge such as: "it's not X, it's Y", "dvelve", "byte-order", minimal em-dashes and so forth.

I even came across [Fabien Sanglard](https://fabiensanglard.net/)'s code style rules from [this HackerNews thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48884313) that I have enjoyed incorporating as well. Why not have my agent write code that mimics engineers I admire!?

Despite this, I still found my agents continuing to use these banned words and phrases. 😒

## Reproducible AI-slop removal

I was _a little annoyed_ I had to keep re-prompting my preferred writing style but I often reflect on [the bitter lesson](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html) and generally try to avoid any additional work while I wait for the model's capabilities to improve.

I was however surprised when upon one such occasion I realized that the agent itself had itself gotten frustrated at forgetting my writing style and turned it into a flake check.

[![scooby doo code comment meme](/assets/images/scooby_doo_code_comment_meme.png)](/assets/images/scooby_doo_code_comment_meme.png)
{: style="--image-width: 20rem"}

During the creation of [omnibin.dev](https://omnibin.dev), I noticed the agent had created [tools/check-prose.py](https://github.com/fzakaria/omnibin/blob/fc56e228eaf26407d976d59f4dcda306ead87345/tools/check-prose.py) to check for banned words and phrases in the source tree. It is a simple Python script that walks the repo and fails on a banned word, phrase, or a single em dash anywhere.

I had begun setting up a distinct pattern of how I like my repositories laid out so it was funny to see it automatically include this check in the CI of my build.

Despite the _bitter lesson_, I might try leveraging this check in my other repos. What I like about deterministic checks rather than a markdown context-file is that it's verifyable and reproducible. I'm not left guessing whether the agent is following my style or not. I can run the check myself and see if it passes or fails.

## Honesty is a mirror

Let's be honest with myself and do some introspection. How much has AI-isms crept into my writing? I applied the same check to my own blog posts.

| Years     | Posts | Em dashes | Banned words | Banned phrases |
|-----------|------:|----------:|-------------:|---------------:|
| 2020-2024 |    64 |         0 |           17 |             13 |
| 2025-2026 |    91 |        21 |           54 |             16 |

Clearly I was not a fan of em dashes in the last five years, then suddenly I found 21 uses in two, which happen to coincide with the proliferation of AI.[^leverage] 🤔

[^leverage]: The top offender is `leverage` at 51 hits. I used it in 2021 and 2022 too. Some tells are just mine.

We seem to be all worried about how many em dashes are in our text. [Goodhart's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodhart%27s_law) teaches us that at that point, that is no longer a good measure of AI slop.

Treat each other kindly.
